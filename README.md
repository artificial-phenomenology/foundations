# Artificial Phenomenology

Artificial phenomenology is the interventionist study of stable, reportable,
and causally structured first-person-like organization in artificial systems,
without presupposing that those systems are conscious.

This repository holds the founding documents of the Artificial Phenomenology
Research Lab: what the lab is for, how it runs an experiment, and what it plans
to do next.

The lab is run by Leo Ferres.

## The documents

| Document | What it is | Changes? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [MANIFESTO.md](MANIFESTO.md) | Why the lab exists and what it will and will not claim | Rarely, and never quietly |
| [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) | The minimum standard an experiment has to meet | Yes, as the lab learns |
| [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) | What is finished and what is running | Whenever an experiment changes |

The manifesto is the constitution. Changing it requires a dated reason, and
every earlier version stays available. The other two are working documents and
are expected to change. Each carries a version number and a date.

## How this work is done

This is AI-native research. AI may contribute at every stage, from framing
questions and writing code to analysis, critique, and writing. Leo remains
fully accountable for everything published. The full statement of practice is
at [ai-native-practice](https://github.com/leoferres/ai-native-practice).

## Experiments

Experimental reports are published separately, one repository each, so that a
finished study can be frozen and cited without being edited afterwards.

- Experimental Report 001, *Autobiographical Memory Transplantation in an
  Artificial Agent*

[EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) lists what is finished and what is running.

## Criticism and correction

Reproduction, criticism, alternative explanations, and failed replications are
all welcome, and failed replications are as publishable here as successful
ones. Open an issue in the relevant repository.

Errors get corrected in public, with a date and a note saying what changed.
